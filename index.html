01 / About

Research that keeps widening the question.

I am completing a B.Sc. in Biotechnology & Genetic Engineering at Sylhet Agricultural University. My research began with computational biology, then expanded into fisheries genetics, wetland conservation, field research and current microbiology.

I tend to follow a question across scales. A molecular pattern can lead to a population question, and a field observation can lead to a microbial one. That cumulative path is now shaping how I think about aquatic health.

02 / Research Journey

How one question led to the next.

Eight stages, shown as a research path rather than a list of projects.

01

Molecular scale

Molecular & Computational Biology

Multi step computational work taught me to connect several analyses before trusting a biological interpretation.

Docking, molecular dynamics, evidence integration, scientific visualization

I began with computational biology, drug design and bioinformatics. The PLOS ONE collaboration later gave that early interest a demanding workflow where structure, docking, dynamics and visualization had to support the same biological story.

02

A wider scale

Turning Toward Conservation

Conservation shifted the scale of the questions I wanted to ask, from isolated mechanisms to populations and ecosystems.

Conservation framing, literature synthesis, Hilsa as a focal species

Watching David Attenborough: A Life on Our Planet sharpened a conservation interest that had already been growing. Hilsa became especially compelling because its migratory life history links marine, estuarine and riverine systems with fisheries and livelihoods.

03

Population scale

Hilsa Genetics, Ecology & Conservation

Genetic variation became more meaningful when I began asking about migration, habitat connectivity and fishing pressure.

cytb genetics, BioEdit, BLAST, DnaSP, MEGA11, phylogenetic interpretation

Sequence analysis moved my thinking from molecular interaction to population variation. Genetics showed a pattern, but ecology raised the next question: what environmental and management forces might be shaping it?

04

Human dimension

Ecosystem Services & Research Instruction

Working with ecosystem service ideas and teaching research methods made the human side of conservation impossible to ignore.

Ecosystem services, fisheries policy, research instruction, evidence synthesis

Fishing bans, livelihoods, ecosystem services and policy started to appear as parts of the same conservation problem. Teaching also helped me connect ideas that had previously felt separate.

05

Field reality

Community Facing Field Research

Fieldwork made policy, implementation and livelihood questions much less abstract.

Endline fieldwork, respondent communication, stakeholder interpretation

Community facing work showed how interventions are experienced outside the clean structure of a paper. Research instruction gave me conceptual connections; fieldwork made me test them against lived evidence.

06

Biodiversity scale

Fish Genetic Resources of Bangladesh

Broadening from Hilsa to fish genetic resources showed how species richness, threat status and wetland differences fit into conservation decisions.

Biodiversity assessment, IUCN status, Shannon and Margalef indices, QGIS, SPSS

This work widened the system beyond one focal species. Comparing wetland biodiversity and conservation context made me think more directly about how habitats, pressures and management priorities interact.

07

Inside the fish

The Hilsa Gut Microbiome Question

Interest in fish health brought the question back inside the fish: what role might microbial communities play at the fish and environment interface?

Host microbe thinking, feasibility assessment, sampling and preservation planning

I first imagined a sequencing heavy microbiome project, but infrastructure, storage, logistics and budget made that difficult to execute. Exploring culture dependent alternatives taught me that a strong question and an executable project are not always the same thing.

08

Current convergence

AMR, Fish Market Microbiology & One Health

Current fish market microbiology made the microbial and environmental dimensions of aquatic health concrete.

Field sampling, selective culture, AST, ESBL confirmation, DNA, PCR, SPSS

This thesis brought earlier habits together: careful workflow design, molecular thinking, ecological context, human interfaces and feasibility. It has made me increasingly interested in aquatic health problems that sit between environment, fish, microbes and people.

Where this is leading

Environmental stressors, fish associated microbiota, fish health and conservation.

Hilsa remains a particularly compelling focal system within that broader direction.
