Fish-market microbiology | Undergraduate thesis

Linked interfaces, AMR and evidence boundaries

Problem: understand how presumptive contamination and resistance appeared across fish, water, handlers, tools and processing surfaces rather than treating each source in isolation.

Approach: linked sampling, culture and biochemical characterization, AST, attempted molecular confirmation and cautious interpretation when PCR confirmation was not achieved.

Hilsa research | Conservation systems

Connecting genetics, ecology and management

Problem: move beyond a single biological pattern and ask what migration, habitat, fishing pressure, ecosystem services and management mean together for Hilsa conservation.

Approach: combine population-level evidence with ecological and management context, then communicate the connection through figures, analysis and manuscript development.

How I approach a project

Start with the core question, not the software.

01

Understand

Clarify what the project is actually trying to answer, who the audience is and what the output needs to do.

02

Check the evidence

Separate what the data and sources support from what would be an overclaim or an unsupported assumption.

03

Build the structure

Organize the analysis, narrative or visual so each part has a clear job and the logic is easy to follow.

04

Communicate

Turn the work into a manuscript section, figure, map, slide, poster or visual that is accurate and easy to understand.

Academic integrity

Clear boundaries are part of the service.

I support researchers without replacing authorship or inventing evidence. I do not offer thesis ghostwriting, fabricated data or analysis, guaranteed journal acceptance, fake peer review or services that misrepresent a client's contribution. The goal is to strengthen legitimate research work and communicate it more clearly.
