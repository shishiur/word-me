SLIDE 3 - Q1 - the same place is a different resource in every season
target 1:10 | 171 words | ~70 s at 145 wpm
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SAY:

Start with the calendar, because everything else hangs off it.

We arrived thinking of Tanguar Haor as a place. We left thinking of it as a sequence.

Top band: for roughly five months it is deep open water. Then it recedes. Then, for four months, much of it is a crop field. Same ground, same people.

Now what sits on top. The fishing ban is three monsoon months, said to apply everywhere. The society's lease window is a different three months, in the dry season. The embankment closes in Chaitra. Tourist boats come in the dry season. Labour migration fills the gaps.

So at any moment the haor produces a different thing, for a different group, under a different rule — and the fisher, the paddy farmer and the boat operator are often the same household in different months.

Professor Nayak defines ephemeral commons as a commons whose own process of creation produces instability, cycling through creation and dissolution. Here that is literal. One caution: every band is reported, not measured.

SLIDE 4 - Q1 - attributes, and what we now ask
target 1:00 | 147 words | ~60 s at 145 wpm
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SAY:

Four attributes we could point to.

Emergence and submergence, which you have just seen. Access that changes with the season rather than with who you are. Subtractability rising as the water falls. And exclusion that is costly and partial: of about fifty short machines, one participant said two get caught, are fined, and released.

Now the shift. We went in thinking the ephemeral quality was about water. What unsettled that was this line at twenty-seven twenty-one. He describes the lease era, when the lessee kept armed guards, as the time when fish were plentiful. Less than a minute later he says the present arrangement suits them.

We are not going to resolve that contradiction. It is the finding. The institution governing this commons has itself been ephemeral — estate, lease, lease-free since 2000, society windows now. The rule dissolves and reappears as surely as the water does.

[Pause. Hand to Speaker 2.]

The community's account of the lessee era: 'there were ten guns on this Tanguar beel' (00:27:21), and one to two crore taka of katha bamboo with fifty to a hundred guards (00:49:03).

SLIDE 5 - Q2 - the embankment: one structure, two opposite effects
target 1:35 | 230 words | ~95 s at 145 wpm
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SAY:

The clearest thing we encountered was the embankment, and it shows why a one-day diagnosis would have been wrong.

Top left. The community asked for it; a CBRMP project built it. They credit it with ten to twelve years of harvests and with flood water no longer reaching the homesteads. Had we stopped there, we would have written a success story.

Top right, same conversation. In Chaitra the embankment is closed and the field dries. When the field dries, they said, fish cannot survive. When it breaks, in Magh or Falgun, water comes in and fish spawn again. In his own logic: if the water stays deep, then however much people fish illegally, they cannot finish the stock.

So the same structure protects the paddy and removes what the fish need. It is not one group gaining and another losing. It is often the same household, in different months.

Bottom left is our reading, dashed on the figure for that reason. Silt settling behind an embankment is a mechanism we did not measure.

Bottom right is the line that stayed with us. 'Right now we are surviving, that is fine, but for the future...' He does not finish the sentence.

Professor Nayak said on the eighth: despite best engineering, a dam breaks, and that is uncertainty. The community already holds both halves of that. Nobody had to teach them non-linearity.

IF ASKED (not part of the timed run):
Siltation in the community's words: the turbid flood brings mountain soil, it fills up, 'the haor is rising up' (00:44:40). The embankment future: 'the kanda is thin now; two or four more cuts and all of it becomes a mound' (00:45:44). CBRMP named by the mentor in our group discussion at 00:05:08.

SLIDE 6 - Q2 - drivers across levels, and who is said to cause them
target 1:20 | 199 words | ~82 s at 145 wpm
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SAY:

Two points, because the figure does the work.

First, scale. The CNRS informant traces sand and silt to coal and limestone extraction in the Meghalaya hills upstream. A community speaker described the same thing from the other end: the turbid water brings mountain soil, and, in his words, the haor is rising up. Big boats can no longer reach where they once could. At the other end, land ownership sits with one ministry and custodianship with another, and tourism investment comes from Dhaka.

Professor Nayak called that mismatch governance misfit.

Second, the part that corrected us. We expected to hear that outsiders destroy the fishery. Asked directly who uses the shortgun and the fine-mesh kona jal, the answer at forty-nine thirty-seven was: people from here, people of this very Tanguar.

And they do not agree with each other about which gear is the problem. The speaker who insists the current net does not destroy fish has used it since birth, for food; he names the short machine and the kona jal instead. Another names the china duari.

We are keeping that disagreement, because resolving it would mean choosing one fisher's account over another's with nothing to decide it on.

[Pause. Hand to Speaker 3.]

SLIDE 7 - Q3 - food and income
target 1:30 | 221 words | ~91 s at 145 wpm
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SAY:

My half of question three is food and income.

What we saw is not general poverty. It is exposure with a shape.

Catch is described as half a kilo now, against plenty before. Then the market fails them twice. Going to town costs two hundred to two hundred and fifty taka and takes two hours, so a small catch cannot pay the fare. Buyers come to the village instead, called nekari, and participants said the price is not fair. For dried fish: three hundred taka a kilo in the market, three to five hundred to get there, so they sell at two fifty locally.

The paddy stays home. It is the year's eating, not a cash crop.

One honest qualification. We saw rice stored in empty fish-feed sacks. Our mentor read that as stockpiling against uncertainty, and it is persuasive. But the storage is the observation; the reason is our inference.

Why is this an ephemeral-commons vulnerability rather than just low income? Because the income is not low steadily. It is concentrated into a window, then absent. The household absorbs the variability of the resource in its own calendar.

Then this, at thirty-three minutes, on why awareness campaigns do not stop fishing. The CNRS informant made the same point independently: without food security, awareness alone will not hold a conservation rule.

IF ASKED (not part of the timed run):
Market figures from the group discussion at 00:01:06-00:01:33 and from the interview at 00:40:14-00:40:44. Women's reported work: cutting and drying shutki, cooking, sorting fish; they do not make or repair nets, and net-making stopped when the current net arrived (00:41:13, 00:41:29).

SLIDE 9 - Q3 - water, schooling, health, shelter
target 1:35 | 234 words | ~96 s at 145 wpm
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SAY:

My argument is one sentence: in all four of these services something has been provided, and the provision is not what determines whether people are safe.

Water. Three tubewells reported out of order. One submersible serves the kanda at twenty taka a month. Real provision. But piped water is used for drinking and cooking, not bathing, and river water is still preferred because, as they put it, the tubewell water has no taste. And we do not know whether the water people drink is safe. We did not test it; nobody we met had. Our group discussed skin irritation and menstrual hygiene when the water is turbid, but that is an association someone described, not a cause anyone established.

Health. Tahirpur is first aid; childbirth means Sunamganj. A participant said of the monsoon, in three words, that it is death. Travel is by hand-rowed boat. We were told of a maternal death in transport, but that reached us through our own discussion, so we present it as second-hand.

Shelter. Tin roofs, no windbreak, wind off the hills across open water. The only candidate storm shelter is the school across three rivers — which in a signal warning is exactly when you cannot cross.

Professor Nayak read the locally established madrasa as possible self-organization. We find that persuasive as a reading. It is not evidence that children are being educated, and we did not look.

SLIDE 11 - Q4 bridge - responses across levels
target 1:15 | 176 words | ~72 s at 145 wpm
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SAY:

One housekeeping note. Our announced split covers questions one, two, three, five and six. Question four had no presenter, so I am bridging it here.

Three kinds of actor. The community is organised: forty-one societies hold the lease. But disputes are settled by the matbor and the elders, not the society. They established a madrasa. Three villages are reported to run a lottery for turns with the tourist boats, which is a rule about exclusion that nobody wrote down.

Government is present mainly as announcement. Participants described being told fishing is prohibited; they did not describe being told why a gear is harmful. One volunteered that people who do not fish at all hold fisher cards.

Civil society is present as a sequence of projects with end dates.

The thing I would point at sits underneath all three. Legal ownership of the land is with the Ministry of Land; custodianship is with Environment and Forests. Custody and ownership are not in the same hands. That is governance misfit in the structure, not anyone's failure to try.

SLIDE 12 - Q5 - coping, adapting, or transitioning?
target 1:35 | 233 words | ~96 s at 145 wpm
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SAY:

Question five asks whether vulnerability-to-viability transitions are taking place. The honest answer from one day: we can describe the first two columns and we cannot fill the third.

Coping is well evidenced. Rice in fish-feed sacks. Potatoes when fish are scarce. Paracetamol and antacid at home. Selling to the nekari because the market is unreachable. Moving to Netrokona when the water reached neck height in 2022.

Adaptive response is also evidenced, and more interesting, because some of it is new. Small-boat hire at twenty to fifty taka a head; an NGO gave boats free and people work them for hire. Lease, then paddy, then other work. Seasonal labour to Tangail and Sylhet, permanent migration to Dhaka. A locally established madrasa. NGO loans in women's names, though the men said plainly that it is they who supply the money.

The third column is where I want to be careful. Every item is a condition that would have to hold, not something we observed. Income that lasts the year. A fish stock that recovers. A card that reaches the person it names. We saw none of it established.

The boatman was asked directly whether tourism money lasts the year. He said no, and then talked about going elsewhere for work.

So tourism is a real new income stream and it is not yet a transition. Holding both at once is our answer to question five.

SLIDE 13 - Q5 - the counterexample we were given
target 1:35 | 232 words | ~96 s at 145 wpm
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SAY:

This is where the field corrected us most directly, and it belongs in a reflection more than any success story.

Near the end of the interview one of us put it to the participant that the big boats erode the bank, he has to rebuild it, and therefore the houseboats harm him. Listen to the shape of that: the question contains the conclusion.

His answer, at fifty-eight twenty-four. No, we are getting a benefit from the houseboats. I am seeing cash profit. And he gives the mechanism: a big boat ties up, ten small boats go out, each taking passengers to the watch tower at twenty or fifty taka a head.

He had refused the same framing ten minutes earlier, naming the kona jal and the short machine instead. His one complaint about tourists was the polythene.

The CNRS informant says close to the opposite: tourism grew suddenly, houseboats pressure the environment, and local people are not getting the benefit expected.

We will not adjudicate that. Both accounts can hold, because they are measured over different horizons. The benefit is immediate, in cash, in one household. The cost is diffuse, slower, borne by a system. The person best placed to describe today's cash is not the person best placed to feel a cost arriving in a decade.

That is a question about scale and about time, not a disagreement about what happened.

SLIDE 14 - Q5 - what evidence is missing
target 1:35 | 235 words | ~97 s at 145 wpm
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SAY:

If we are going to claim a transition, we should say what would count as evidence. First, one comparison we are refusing.

An earlier government assessment is reported to have recorded a hundred and forty-one fish species. CNRS fish-catch monitoring, two months before our conversation, found sixty-eight. It is tempting, and we have seen it done, to subtract and announce seventy-three extinctions.

We will not. The numbers come from different methods, windows and scopes. Catch monitoring records what is landed at a market, not what exists in the water. We report both and refuse the subtraction.

The same applies to the turtle one speaker has not seen in ten years while another saw one in 2022, and to the elders describing flocks of birds that looked like cloud. Those are memories offered as memories. CNRS told us no continuous species-level dataset exists for this place.

So, what would have to be true. Income holding through the nine months outside the lease window, for named households, over more than one year. A fish stock measured the same way twice, far enough apart to show direction. Entitlements that actually arrive. A reason to expect gear use to change, given that the users are local and expect to fish here again. And somebody accountable at the level where the driver sits, not only where the damage shows.

None of that is a day's work. That is the point.

[Pause. Hand to Speaker 7.]

IF ASKED (not part of the timed run):
CNRS Part 02 on the absent dataset and on training local people in species identification. Bird hunting economics, which we did not raise with the community: a poisoned bird sells at 400-500 taka, a live-caught bird at 1,200-1,500, and fifteen to twenty days of catching can bring one to one and a half lakh taka - which is why the informant said awareness and one-off payments do not stop it.

SLIDE 15 - Q6 - three conditional futures
target 1:35 | 221 words | ~91 s at 145 wpm
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SAY:

I want to answer question six as three conditional futures rather than a recommendation, because a recommendation from one day is exactly what we were warned against.

First, if nothing changes upstream. Silt keeps arriving, depth keeps falling, and the three-month window keeps producing less. Who would have to act? Someone with reach upstream of this haor. Nobody we met had that reach, and that is the finding, not an accusation.

Second, if the local proposal is taken up. It is theirs and it is specific: deepen the beel, and put the katha bamboo back as sanctuary. They have watched the lessees do it, with one to two crore taka of bamboo and fifty to a hundred guards.

Third, if tourism grows without management. Immediate cash for the villages with boats. Polythene, bank erosion, and on the CNRS account, pressure without the local benefit expected.

Now the tension I am not going to resolve. When they ask for a sanctuary, the stated reason is so there will be more fish to catch. But it is the rabbit and the carrots from the lecture: plenty of food will not solve the problem, and plenty of food could be a problem.

Whether a sanctuary built to increase catch functions as a sanctuary is a real question. It is theirs to settle, not ours.

IF ASKED (not part of the timed run):
The local proposal is at 00:43:50-00:44:34: deepen the beel by government order, and katha bamboo so that 'when there are more fish we will catch more'. CNRS plans: re-excavation of one or two silted beels, a fish pass on the old Nazarkhali to Hatirgata channel, ecotourism rules, tourist pre-registration on the St Martin's model, an ecosystem service fee, and alternative income for about 400 households. A conflict the informant named: commercial duck rearing is possible but ducks in a jalmahal can damage fish eggs, and a lessee may not permit them.
