Hilsa molecular genetics concept board

Two directions for the cytochrome b study of hilsa in the Sylhet haor and river system. Published values come from Oceans 2026 and EJABF 2025; drawn elements are labelled.

P02 · Direction A

Silver Thread

Scale is the story: one silver fish, one mitochondrial gene, 1,114 aligned positions. The visitor travels inward, and the sample size travels with them.

Selected: core

one fish, one gene

1,114 aligned positions

N N N N · n = 3 PER SITE

Newsreader italic for scale captions, Plex Mono for positions, Instrument Sans for reading.

Material & light

Specular silver on a dark field. Soft-light bands of violet and gold cross the flank the way hilsa scales shift colour. The alignment is lit like a filament.

Motion

  • Zoom fish → mitogenome: 900 ms
  • Magnifier follows the scrub with an 80 ms lag
  • Iridescence shifts with pointer only, never on a loop

Interaction

  • Scrub the 1,114 positions (pointer or arrow keys)
  • Tap the fish to see what was sampled
  • Toggle “what n = 3 can show”

Build

SVG for fish, ring and thread. Optional WebGL iridescence on the fish only, as a progressive layer that is off under reduced motion.

Integrity safeguards

  • N for every unloaded base
  • 15 variable sites shown as an unplaced count
  • n = 3 badge pinned for the whole journey

Evidence it uses

Published Oceans 2026 cytb figures; Awaiting evidence alignment FASTA and accessions.

Strengths

  • Makes the molecular method tangible without inventing data
  • Shows the small sample size in the same frame as the result
  • Distinct from the Salmonella bench and the wetland landscapes

Risks

  • Thin until the FASTA arrives; the magnifier is all N
  • Hides the landscape that gives the stock question meaning
  • The zoom can feel like a cliché if overplayed

P02 · Direction B

Monsoon Confluence

The haor and the river in flood season, rendered as moving water. Two sampled sites, three fish each, set in the landscape that makes “one well-connected stock” plausible.

Map kept; water rejected

Hakaluki Haor

Surma River

SCHEMATIC · NO ROUTES

Newsreader italic for place names, Plex Mono for map furniture.

Material & light

Flow-noise water from a fragment shader beneath a cartographic overlay at 80% opacity. Dusk light, paper-map hairlines.

Motion

  • Water drifts at a few pixels per second
  • No directional currents or arrows
  • Badges expand on select: 300 ms

Interaction

  • Choose a site
  • Read the FST range on Wright’s scale
  • Switch map labels on or off

Build

About 30 lines of GLSL with no library, plus the SVG map. Static gradient fallback.

Integrity safeguards

  • “No routes implied” printed on the map
  • Site-level badges, not sampling points
  • FST range attributed to EJABF 2025

Evidence it uses

Published two sites, n = 3 (Oceans), FST 0.000–0.017 (EJABF).

Strengths

  • Atmospheric and unmistakably Sylhet
  • Puts the genetics in the place it is about
  • Technically light: one shader, no framework

Risks

  • Moving water visually argues for connectivity, the very claim n = 3 can only weakly support
  • Maps invite false precision about sampling points that are not published
  • Shader cost on low-end phones

Silver Thread is the core; the map opens it.

The static map establishes Hakaluki Haor and the Surma with their n = 3 badges, then the experience moves inward to the fish, the mitogenome and the 1,114-position thread. The FST lens closes it, with Wright’s bands as reference science and the sample-size warning beside the result.

The WebGL water stays in the board as a tested technique, not the default: moving water persuades the eye of connectivity before the visitor reads the evidence.
