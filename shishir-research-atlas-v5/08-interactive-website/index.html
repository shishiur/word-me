P01-S03 · Selective culture and biochemistry

From 154 samples to 40 isolates

On xylose lysine deoxycholate agar, Salmonella typically grows as red colonies with black centres: it ferments xylose, decarboxylates lysine, which returns the medium to red, and its hydrogen sulphide turns the centre black.

Reference science

Choose a colony type to isolate it on the plate. Black centres mark hydrogen sulphide, which is why look-alikes such as Citrobacter and Proteus are removed by biochemical testing.

P01-S04 · Molecular confirmation Disputed

One gene, two accounts

invA sits in Salmonella pathogenicity island 1 and is a standard Salmonella-specific PCR target. Whether these 40 isolates carried it decides how every later number is described.

Researcher’s latest record provisional

All 40 retained isolates were invA PCR- and gel-confirmed, according to the researcher’s latest record. The Atlas treats this as provisional until the gel photograph, controls and final thesis are checked.

Portfolio text, August 2026

None of the 40 biochemically compatible isolates produced an interpretable invA-specific PCR product, according to the portfolio text published in August 2026. The findings stay presumptive.

Isolates are now called invA-positive isolates (provisional).

P01-S05 · Antimicrobial susceptibility

Reading resistance on Mueller–Hinton

Each of the 40 isolates was lawned onto Mueller–Hinton agar with antibiotic discs; twelve agents were tested and nine interpreted (P01-E011). Under the current account they are invA-positive isolates (provisional).
