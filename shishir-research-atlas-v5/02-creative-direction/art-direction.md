# Art direction

## Philosophy

Each project is shown in the place where its evidence was made: a cutting station, a lab bench, a haor, a computer. Realism is in service of reading, so every scene follows four rules.

1. **One light, one focal object.** A scene has a single key light and the camera brings one object into it at a time. The light is never decorative; it is the selection device.
2. **Materials tell the method.** Wet wood, steel and bamboo say "market"; glass, agar and white epoxy say "lab". The hand-over between the two is an explicit transition band, not a cut.
3. **Evidence is typographic, not pictorial.** Numbers, sequences, zones and gels are drawn from data by code or not drawn at all. Generated imagery carries environment and anatomy only.
4. **Motion answers a question.** Movement happens when the visitor asks for something (select, step, scrub) or once on arrival. No idle loops.

## What we never show

Gel bands, DNA bases, phylogenies, zone sizes, CFU counts or site coordinates that were not supplied; bacteria colonising tissue; migration routes; identifiable people without consent; any PRCC material before permission; the Pond Water-Quality Assessment.
